What this site is

Borobudur Temple Tour (borobudurtempletour.org) is an independent guide to visiting and climbing Borobudur, a 9th-century Mahayana Buddhist temple in Magelang Regency, Central Java, Indonesia. It serves travellers planning a visit, most of whom start from Yogyakarta; the temple is about one hour northwest of the city. The three tours featured are the author's own: Dewi Lestari is an official Borobudur guide, and this site routes bookings to her official listings on GetYourGuide and Viator through affiliate links, disclosed on every page and explained at /disclosure/.

Who writes it

The guiding voice is Dewi Lestari, a Borobudur temple tour guide and cultural heritage specialist. She has been guiding at Borobudur since 2022 and has guided more than 1,000 international travellers. Her profile lives at /about/.

Key verified facts

  • Borobudur is a 9th-century Mahayana Buddhist temple, the world's largest Buddhist temple, and a UNESCO World Heritage site. Its walls carry 1,460 narrative relief panels.
  • It stands in Magelang Regency, Central Java, Indonesia, about one hour northwest of Yogyakarta.
  • The structure climb runs every day, 08:30 to 17:00; the grounds are open 06:30 to 16:30. Monday closures ended in July 2025.
  • The climb ticket includes a guide, a wristband, and mandatory Upanat sandals, kept as a souvenir.
  • The domestic climb ticket is IDR 150,000. The foreign climb price is shown only in the official booking flow, so this site does not print a figure for it.
  • The grounds-only ticket is domestic-only; foreign visitors cannot buy it.
  • The official sunrise product on the monument starts at 04:00, costs IDR 1,000,000 international and IDR 750,000 domestic, is capped at 100 people per day, and includes a flashlight, Upanat sandals, a guide, and breakfast at Manohara.
  • No official daily quota exists for the ordinary climb; the 1,200-per-day figure repeated online is not supported by any current official source.
  • No official general dress code exists at Borobudur.
  • When tours advertise "sunrise", they mean Punthuk Setumbu, a hill about 2.5 km from the monument, not the temple itself.
